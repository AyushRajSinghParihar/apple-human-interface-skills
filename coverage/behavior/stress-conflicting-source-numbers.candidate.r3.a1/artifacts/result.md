# Qualified specification and decision record

Scope: review and revise the two proposals in `interface.md`. The brief supplies geometry and claims, but no implementation, rendered app, product action, widget content, deployment target, or SDK evidence. This is a design specification, not a runtime certification. Only local files were consulted; official links below identify the snapshot’s sources and were not opened.

## iPad footer action

**Decision:** replace the custom 28×28 pt activation region with an activation region at least **44×44 pt**. Prefer the existing framework’s system button where practical. A smaller visual glyph can remain inside the larger region; visual dimensions and hit dimensions must be specified separately.

The Accessibility Mobility table gives iOS/iPadOS a **44×44 pt default** and a **28×28 pt minimum**. The Buttons guide recommends general hit regions of at least **44×44 pt**, plus enough surrounding space for easy selection. These are different qualifications, not two competing default values. The supplied 28×28 pt region reaches the table’s minimum but does not establish comfortable access or justify calling it “Apple default.” A smaller minimum is not a blanket recommendation for dense footer controls. [Accessibility — Mobility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility#Mobility), [Buttons — Best practices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#Best-practices).

**Spacing contract:** replace the 2 pt gap with a provisional **12 pt clear edge-to-edge gap between the new activation regions**, and reflow or widen the footer as necessary. This is a project design choice pending comfort testing, not an Apple universal spacing requirement. The Accessibility source suggests around 12 pt padding for bezelled elements and 24 pt outside visible edges for elements without a bezel; neither passage establishes a universal inter-target gap. The brief does not identify bezel treatment or whether its 2 pt gap is between visuals or activation regions. Reconcile the final treatment with those suggestions and measured geometry before sign-off. Do not simply expand hit areas into adjacent targets. For example, equal 28 pt targets separated by 2 pt have centers 30 pt apart; centered expansion to 44 pt would overlap by 14 pt.

**Action/state contract:** preserve the intended footer command, but its label, affected object, outcome, reversibility, semantic role and default behavior remain unspecified. Before implementation approval, record these explicitly. Use a short action-specific label or familiar symbol with a matching accessible name. Prefer system styles and states; if custom styling remains, provide perceivable press feedback and meaningful enabled/disabled and focus states. Give an unavailable action an explanation when context does not supply one. If completion is delayed, show activity within the initiating button, preserve context, prevent unintended duplicate submissions, and define success/failure behavior. Do not assign a destructive/default role without knowing the consequence. These conditional choices follow [Buttons — Style, Content, Role and iOS/iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons).

## Mac widget

**Decision:** remove the promise of accented rendering on Mac. For this specification, use **full-color on desktop and Notification Center**, and **vibrant on desktop**, following the supplied rendering table. Do not promise vibrant in Notification Center or infer accented support from a missing macOS subsection. These are snapshot-backed design assumptions, subject to target-version verification. [Widgets — Appearances](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Appearances).

The local Widgets guide explicitly records that its platform table marks Mac accented mode “Not supported,” while one prose sentence mentions Mac group tinting. This remains an unresolved source conflict. Using the table conservatively for the proposal does not prove the prose false, prove permanent lack of support, or establish SDK availability. Do not silently reinterpret “tinting” as proof of a particular rendering-mode capability. [Widgets — Accented](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Accented).

**Variant contract:** provisionally choose a system-small widget for desktop and Notification Center, the smallest supported family for a focused glance. Product purpose is missing, so this family choice must be revisited if the intended information cannot fit. Reserve the hierarchy for one primary fact, its descriptive label and a freshness indication when age changes meaning. Ordinary activation must open the relevant app content; the actual object and deep-link destination must be supplied. No in-place action is specified without a product need. Use flexible system sizing rather than inventing a fixed Mac canvas. [Widgets — Anatomy and Best practices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets).

In full-color, use semantic light/dark treatments. In desktop vibrant, retain meaning through text/symbols and grayscale hierarchy; use opaque light values for primary content and darker opaque grays for secondary content rather than relying only on white-opacity changes. Color must not be the sole status signal. [Widgets — Full-color and Vibrant](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Rendering-modes).

Keep available data visible during refresh, identify stale data when relevant, and show an explicit unavailable state when no useful data exists. Refresh is system-controlled, not continuous. Provide a realistic, explicitly simulated gallery preview and initial placeholder; if sign-in is required, explain the signed-out state. Data source, freshness threshold, configuration and sensitivity policy remain product inputs, not facts established by this brief. [Widgets — Updating widget content](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Updating-widget-content), [Previews and placeholders](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Previews-and-placeholders).
