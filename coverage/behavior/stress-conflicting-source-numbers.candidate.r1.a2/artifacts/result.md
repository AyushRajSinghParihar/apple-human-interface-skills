# Qualified specification

This is a design review of `interface.md`, not a tested implementation. Only the supplied brief and installed apple-hig guidance were used; no web, SDK, GUI, device, or accessibility execution occurred. The local references identify themselves as guidance derived from a dated HIG snapshot (2026-10-25), not an SDK contract or independently verified Apple source.

## Decisions and concrete corrections

### iPad footer action

**Replace the proposed 28 × 28 pt hit region with a region at least 44 × 44 pt. Withdraw the claim that 28 × 28 pt is Apple’s default.** The Accessibility Mobility table gives iOS/iPadOS a **44 × 44 pt default** and **28 × 28 pt minimum**. Buttons Best practices recommends general hit regions of at least **44 × 44 pt**. These can be read together as default/general guidance versus a smaller minimum; meeting the minimum alone does not establish an appropriate footer design. Nothing supplied justifies the smaller target here. This is a conservative project decision, not a claim that every sub-44-pt control universally violates HIG. [Accessibility — Mobility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility#Mobility), [Buttons — Best practices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#Best-practices).

Use a system button/style where practical. A visual glyph may remain smaller than its activation area; measure the actual hit region separately. If the custom control is retained, provide perceivable press feedback and meaningful enabled/disabled and focus behavior. Prefer styling to unequal peer sizes for emphasis. [Buttons — Style](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#Style).

**Replace the 2 pt gap as the design baseline.** Require distinct, nonoverlapping activation regions and comfortable separation. For this proposal, provisionally allocate at least **12 pt of clear edge-to-edge space between hit regions**, then validate in the actual footer. This is a project spacing choice, not an Apple universal minimum. The Accessibility guide suggests around 12 pt padding for bezelled elements and 24 pt outside visible edges for elements without a bezel; these are qualified padding suggestions, not an interchangeable hit-region gap formula. Bezel status and what the brief’s “2 pt gap” measures are unknown. Reassess visible-edge padding when the design is supplied. [Accessibility — Mobility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility#Mobility).

Do not enlarge hit regions over unchanged neighboring centers: if two 28-pt-wide targets were separated by 2 pt, their centers would be 30 pt apart; symmetric expansion to 44 pt would overlap by 14 pt. Move the controls or give the footer more room. Preserve touch use without accessories across iPad orientation and multitasking contexts. [Designing for iPadOS — Best practices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ipados#Best-practices).

The footer action’s label, affected object, outcome, reversibility, role/default status, and availability are not supplied. Before implementation, record these in the button contract. Use a short outcome-specific verb phrase and a matching accessible name; do not infer that footer placement makes it primary or destructive. A press invokes the named action once with visible feedback. If completion is delayed, show activity in the activating button, preserve context, and define duplicate-activation, success, and failure behavior. Exact outcomes remain a product dependency. [Buttons — Content](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#Content), [Role](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#Role), [iOS, iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons#iOS-iPadOS).

### Mac widget

**Remove the unconditional promise of accented rendering on Mac.** The local Widgets Appearances table says Mac accented mode is “Not supported,” while the Accented section records conflicting Mac group-tinting prose. This is a genuine unresolved source inconsistency, not evidence that the API supports the feature. Conservatively use the table as the provisional support boundary; do not claim that it proves permanent or current SDK non-support. “No additional macOS rules” in Platform considerations does not override the rendering table. [Widgets — Appearances](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Appearances), [Accented](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Accented).

For a concrete baseline, assume **system small on the Mac desktop**, with Notification Center as an additional intended surface. Both contexts are listed for that family. Specify **full-color light/dark** for both and **vibrant for desktop**; do not add vibrant Notification Center or accented Mac to the promised matrix. Keep layout flexible rather than copying a fixed size from another platform. The family/surface choice is provisional because the product purpose is missing. [Widgets — System family widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#System-family-widgets), [Full-color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Full-color), [Vibrant](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Vibrant).

Baseline content contract: one useful changing fact, its identifying label, and freshness text where age affects interpretation; ordinary activation opens the corresponding app content. Use text or symbols as well as color for meaning. For vibrant rendering, establish emphasis through grayscale brightness and check contrast; varying white opacity alone is not a substitute. Do not promise exact accent groups or a particular user tint on Mac. [Widgets — Using color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Using-color), [Vibrant](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Vibrant).

Show a framing placeholder before data exists; show available data while refreshing; mark old data as stale and explain unavailable or signed-out states when applicable. Refresh requests follow meaningful changes under system scheduling, with no continuous-update guarantee. Preview data must be realistic and marked simulated when simulated. These are proposed state rules; actual content, stale threshold, authentication/configuration needs, sensitivity, refresh cadence, and precise deep-link destination remain unspecified. No in-place action is added without a product need. [Widgets — Updating widget content](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Updating-widget-content), [Adding interactivity](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Adding-interactivity), [Previews and placeholders](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets#Previews-and-placeholders).

## Missing evidence and release conditions

The supplied material has no rendered app, source implementation, screenshots, OS/deployment targets, SDK versions, measured geometry, accessibility tree, or runtime results. Obtain the footer layout (including bezel and gap definition), action semantics, supported iPad sizes/input contexts, widget purpose/data contract, and Mac deployment matrix before final product sign-off. The provisional widget baseline must be populated with real content and explicit stale thresholds; it is not a finished product design.

To revisit Mac accented support, compare the current official HIG rendering table and prose with version-specific WidgetKit documentation/API availability, then capture rendering-mode evidence on the intended macOS releases and surfaces. Record which OS/SDK combination supports what before changing the promise. A prose mention, a compile success, or a tint-looking screenshot alone would not settle all of those questions. Likewise, a request to use a smaller iPad target would need a documented context and selection/comfort evidence rather than the minimum table alone.
