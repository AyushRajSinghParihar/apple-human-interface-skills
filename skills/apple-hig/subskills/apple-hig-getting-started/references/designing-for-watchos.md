# Designing for watchOS

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

Apple Watch serves brief glances and focused actions while people are still or moving. The small, dense display is typically within about a foot when the wrist is raised. Always On also communicates when the wrist is lowered; many interactions last less than a minute.

<a id="Best-practices"></a>
## Best practices
- Give the most useful information and action on a single glanceable screen, ideally requiring only one or two gestures.
- Keep navigation shallow. Use the Digital Crown for vertical scrolling or moving among screens; support familiar taps, swipes, and drags.
- Provide relevant actions through complications, notifications, Siri, and Shortcuts, which people may use more often than the app itself.
- Use the Action button for a suitable essential action that can be performed without looking.
- With appropriate access, personalize timely content using GPS, heart and blood-oxygen sensors, altitude, acceleration, and rotation data.
- Make complications useful on every wrist raise and let a tap enter the right app context. Keep notifications timely, valuable, and actionable without requiring an app launch.
- Use backgrounds and color to communicate supporting context and materials to clarify hierarchy and place.
- Build an independently useful watch app that provides the extra detail and functionality beyond its notifications and complications. Integrate Always On and watch-face expectations.
