# Design principles

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

Use the eight principles as decision criteria when goals compete. Start with how people think, feel, and act; there is no single prescribed visual result.

<a id="Purpose"></a>
## Purpose
Define the valuable outcome the product exists to deliver. Concentrate effort on the features people actually need and make those excellent. Research existing solutions, then identify a useful distinction instead of duplicating them without a reason.

<a id="Agency"></a>
## Agency
Take people directly to their work or content. Allow exploration beyond one prescribed sequence. If guidance is necessary, provide a clear escape or skip. Make actions reversible and restore previous states without losing effort; recovery encourages confident exploration.

<a id="Responsibility"></a>
## Responsibility
Explain the product’s intent, permission requests, collection practices, and reasons for collecting data from the first interaction. Collect only necessary information, protect its integrity, and anticipate misuse, abuse, and unintended harms.

<a id="Familiarity"></a>
## Familiarity
Use recognizable physical and digital concepts. Apply the same appearance and behavior to equivalent controls throughout the experience. Give clear, consistent feedback about availability, changes, progress, alerts, and choices so people can understand and control events.

<a id="Flexibility"></a>
## Flexibility
Account for different abilities, cultures, perspectives, circumstances, devices, and input preferences from the beginning. Preserve context and predictable control locations when adapting layouts; use natural transitions to connect states. Support voice, touch, keyboard, and other appropriate inputs, and give every supported platform deliberate attention.

<a id="Simplicity"></a>
## Simplicity
Keep the experience focused and useful; visual sparseness alone is insufficient. Make essentials readily available, express labels and concepts concisely, and establish a recognizable hierarchy that explains location, next actions, and outcomes.

<a id="Craft"></a>
## Craft
Treat visuals, language, audio, animation, and behavior as expressions of care. Prototype early, test new approaches, discard weak options, and iterate against a high standard. Test in real settings for reliability, performance, and durability. Continue updating the design as platform capabilities and conventions evolve after release.

<a id="Delight"></a>
## Delight
Choose emotions appropriate to the activity: calm, energy, suspense, or another intentional response. Let small interactions and error handling express the product’s character without distracting from tasks. Balance practical refinement and playfulness according to context. A satisfying whole grows from usefulness, freedom, safety, familiarity, adaptability, and care across every moment.
