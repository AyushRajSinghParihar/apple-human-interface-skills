# Designing for iOS

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

An iPhone experience must work both during brief checks on the move and through extended games, browsing, or media sessions. People switch among apps frequently.

<a id="Best-practices"></a>
## Best practices
- Design for a medium-sized, dense display viewed at roughly 1–2 feet, usually held in one or both hands. Accommodate portrait and landscape use.
- Keep primary content and actions clear, with secondary operations reachable through a small number of interactions rather than many simultaneous controls.
- Support touch gestures, onscreen keyboards, and voice; use motion sensors, nearby spatial interactions, or personal information only where they improve the task.
- Put frequent touch controls within comfortable reach, commonly the middle or lower display. Preserve swipe-back navigation and list-row gestures.
- Adapt to orientation, Dark Mode, and Dynamic Type without breaking context.
- With permission, use platform data and services to reduce typing: location, biometric authentication, and payments are examples.
- Integrate appropriate system entry points: widgets, Home Screen quick actions, Spotlight search, Shortcuts, and activity views. Their familiar behavior helps people act outside the main app.
