# Writing

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

Treat language as an integral interface element in onboarding, alerts, accessibility descriptions, and ordinary tasks.

<a id="Getting-started"></a>
## Getting started
Choose a consistent voice based on the audience, familiar vocabulary, and desired feeling; maintain a terminology list. Adjust tone to the situation, such as a serious payment failure versus an achievement celebration, without changing identity. Remove unnecessary words and read copy aloud to test clarity. Use plain, nongendered, localizable wording and consider screen-reader access.

<a id="Best-practices"></a>
## Best practices
- Start each screen with its most important information. Format for scanning and divide unrelated ideas or complex sequences across suitable screens.
- Label actions with clear verbs and links with their actual destination/purpose, avoiding clever substitutes or generic click-here wording. Establish reusable wording and capitalization patterns appropriate to each component type.
- Make multistep flows consistent: entry, continuation, and completion should be clear from labels. Minimize unnecessary my/your possessives and avoid switching perspectives; vague we statements obscure responsibility, especially in errors.
- Name the correct input action for each device. Small watch/phone displays and distant/shared TVs all benefit from brevity; consider everyone who can see a shared screen.
- Give empty states a useful next action, preferably a button/link, without placing essential permanent instructions in a transient state.
- Prevent errors first. When needed, place concise, blame-free messages near the problem and explain a concrete remedy. Avoid flippant interjections and vague invalid-value labels. Reconsider the interaction if many people repeatedly hit the same error.
- Choose alerts, notifications, sheets, or inline guidance according to urgency, context, required action, and explanation length.
- Use practical settings labels. Explain the enabled behavior when necessary and link directly to the setting rather than describing a navigation route.
- Label all text fields. Add format hints/examples and nearby correction guidance that tells people what to enter instead of merely listing prohibitions.
