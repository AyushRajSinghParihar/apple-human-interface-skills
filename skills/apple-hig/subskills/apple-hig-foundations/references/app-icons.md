# App icons

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

An app icon identifies the product in launchers, search, notifications, settings, and sharing contexts. Keep its identity recognizable across platforms and sizes.

<a id="Layer-design"></a>
## Layer design
Layering lets the system add responsive depth. iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and watchOS use a background plus one or more foreground layers with Liquid Glass highlights, refraction, and translucency that can change across releases. tvOS uses 2–5 layers for focus elevation, lighting, sway, and parallax. visionOS uses one background plus 1–2 foreground layers, with shadows and alpha-based embossed depth.

Create foreground artwork in a design tool. For iOS/iPadOS/macOS/watchOS, use Icon Composer to define the background, place layers, tune and preview effects, annotate default/dark/mono variants, and export to Xcode. For tvOS/visionOS, assemble an Xcode image stack and preview parallax with Apple’s tools.

Use crisp foreground edges rather than feathering. Explore translucent overlapping parts to reveal depth; importing opaque shapes then adjusting opacity in Composer helps test interactions with system effects. A simple solid or gradient background usually needs no imported image; imported backgrounds must be opaque and fill the canvas. Prefer SVG or PDF vectors with artwork and text outlined; use lossless PNG for raster content and mesh gradients.

<a id="Icon-shape"></a>
## Icon shape
Supply unmasked square layers for iOS/iPadOS/macOS, which receive rounded-rectangle masks, and for visionOS/watchOS, which receive circular masks. tvOS needs landscape rectangular layers. Pre-masking causes poor highlights and jagged edges. Center essential content, especially inside circular masks, using Apple’s production-template grids.

<a id="Design"></a>
## Design
Choose one distinctive, simple concept with few shapes and a quiet background. Fine detail, thin lines, and sharp corners lose clarity at small sizes or under highlights and shadows. Keep the core design consistent across platforms. Filled overlapping shapes can create useful depth.

Use text only when essential to identity; tiny, unlocalizable icon text is often redundant with the app name. Avoid instructional or temporary words. In tvOS, put essential text on the foreground layer to prevent parallax cropping. Prefer simplified illustrations over detailed photos, screenshots, or replicas of standard controls. Do not reproduce Apple hardware in icons.

<a id="Visual-effects"></a>
## Visual effects
Let the system supply dynamic highlights, shadows between layers, bevels, blur, and glow. If intentional custom effects are necessary, test for conflicts in Icon Composer, Device Hub, and on hardware. Group related layers when an effect should apply to the group; Composer exposes extra Liquid Glass tuning for groups.

<a id="Appearances"></a>
## Appearances
Keep identifying features stable across default, dark, clear, and tinted appearances; missing variants can be generated by the system. Dark designs are subdued and clear/tinted ones more restrained still. Start dark variants from the default palette with complementary colors and restrained brightness; colored backgrounds often improve contrast.

Alternate icons may be offered through app settings on iOS/iPadOS/tvOS and compatible apps in visionOS. Keep alternatives recognizably related to the app. Alternate iOS/iPadOS icons also need dark, clear, and tinted variants; all icons and variants remain subject to app review.

<a id="Platform-considerations"></a>
## Platform considerations
No extra platform rules are listed for iOS, iPadOS, or macOS.

<a id="tvOS"></a>
### tvOS
Leave a safe zone around primary art because focus movement and enlargement crop edges, particularly in foreground layers. The necessary safe zone depends on layer depth, image dimensions, and motion.

<a id="visionOS"></a>
### visionOS
Avoid background shapes intended to look recessed or hollow: system highlights and shadows can make them appear raised.

<a id="watchOS"></a>
### watchOS
Lighten black backgrounds enough to separate the icon from the display’s black surroundings.

<a id="Specifications"></a>
## Specifications
|Platform|Unmasked canvas|System result|Pixels|Construction|Appearance variants|
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
|iOS, iPadOS, macOS|Square|Rounded square|1024 × 1024|Layered|Default, dark, clear light/dark, tinted light/dark|
|tvOS|Landscape rectangle|Rounded rectangle|800 × 480|Layered parallax|No variants listed|
|visionOS|Square|Circle|1024 × 1024|Layered 3D|No variants listed|
|watchOS|Square|Circle|1088 × 1088|Layered|No variants listed|

The system creates smaller settings/notification variants. Supported profiles are sRGB, Gray Gamma 2.2, and Display P3; the source excludes visionOS from its Display P3 icon-support list.
