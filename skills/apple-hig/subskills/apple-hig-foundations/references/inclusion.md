# Inclusion

Source: [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion) · Retrieved 2026-10-25.

This is original application guidance based on the source text. Independent media review is recorded separately in the coverage report.

Inclusive design requires repeated examination of assumptions and evolving understanding of people’s perspectives. Aim for a welcoming experience, beyond merely removing obviously offensive material.

<a id="Inclusive-by-design"></a>
## Inclusive by design
Research goals and experiences with empathy. Consider intersecting age, gender/identity, race/ethnicity, sexuality, physical/cognitive traits, disability, language/culture, religion, education, political/philosophical views, and social/economic circumstances. A word or image can be unclear or carry unintended meaning from another perspective.

<a id="Welcoming-language"></a>
## Welcoming language
Use clear, direct, respectful wording and examine tone from different education and cultural backgrounds. Address people as you/your rather than impersonal user/player; reserve we/our for the company or software without implying an unwanted personal relationship. Define unavoidable specialist terms and keep definitions findable; ordinary words remain easier to read and translate. Replace idioms and culture-bound expressions, especially ones with exclusionary origins. Use humor cautiously because interpretation, translation, and repeated exposure vary.

<a id="Being-approachable"></a>
## Being approachable
Do not require prior expertise. Use straightforward platform-familiar structure and provide learnable onboarding that newcomers can follow while experienced people skip directly to their work.

<a id="Gender-identity"></a>
## Gender identity
Avoid unnecessary gender references and pronouns; neutral nouns and plural constructions can translate more inclusively. Offer customization of avatars, figures, emoji, and characters. Use nongendered figures for generic people. Collect gender only for an actual need such as health/legal purposes; then consider nonbinary, self-description, and decline-to-answer options plus pronouns when necessary for respectful address.

<a id="People-and-settings"></a>
## People and settings
Represent varied races, bodies, ages, abilities, and activities. Avoid stereotyped professions or identities for heroes and enemies. Review settings and possessions too: unnecessary affluence can exclude people. Use relatable places, homes, objects, and activities where the scenario allows.

<a id="Avoiding-stereotypes"></a>
## Avoiding stereotypes
Examine unconscious generalizations about families, capability, culture, or expected life experiences. Do not restrict family structures to one arrangement. Questions assuming college attendance, car ownership, or particular sensory experiences exclude people; prefer experiences and descriptions that do not depend on such assumptions. Check the actual context instead of assuming it is universal.

<a id="Accessibility"></a>
## Accessibility
Support VoiceOver, display accommodations, captions, Switch Control, Speak Screen, and relevant system features. Disability does not imply lack of interest in an activity. Abilities vary across a spectrum, and temporary or situational limitations can affect anyone. Include disabled people in imagery; avoid using disability terms as insults. Describe accomplishments and goals before disability when appropriate and follow a specific person/community’s preferred self-description. Keep interactions simple and information perceivable through multiple senses.

<a id="Languages"></a>
## Languages
Internationalize structure, layout, and date/time/number/currency formatting before localizing words and assets. Respect both language and region preferences. Plain wording, neutral gender references, varied representation, and fewer stereotypes support translation. Use localized/directional SF Symbols and RTL-aware layout. Check color meanings in each locale; one color can convey mourning in one culture and peace in another.

<a id="Platform-considerations"></a>
## Platform considerations
These principles apply across all six platforms without separate platform additions.
