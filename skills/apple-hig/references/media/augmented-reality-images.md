# Augmented reality visual examples

Source: [Apple HIG Augmented reality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/augmented-reality). Snapshot: 2026-10-25.

These original notes describe the inspected images and their primary/alternate appearances. The [main guide](../../subskills/apple-hig-technologies/references/augmented-reality.md) retains the substantive source rules and numerical requirements.

## Scene, coaching, and placement

The Measure example lets the camera scene dominate the screen, with measurement feedback anchored to real carrots and a small stable set of controls around the edges. It demonstrates scene-first composition rather than a dense dashboard over the camera.

The system coaching example dims unrelated detail and uses a simple phone/surface illustration plus a short movement instruction. The app-specific placement indicator appears foreshortened on its detected surface, communicating a place in the scene rather than a flat screen button.

## Manipulation and hints

The direct-interaction diagram shows a finger acting on the object itself. The indirect alternative places separate rotation controls beneath it. Choose the simpler interaction for the circumstances while preserving a useful alternative when people are moving.

Two diagrams illustrate ways to reduce the ambiguity of three-dimensional manipulation: move an object along a detected two-dimensional surface, and rotate it around one vertical axis. The grid and axis arrows explain constrained motion; they do not imply that a single touchscreen gesture should manipulate every degree of freedom simultaneously.

The contextual hint wraps a rotation arrow around the object in the scene. Its text-button counterpart is a separate screen-space control. The labels example points from readable text to two scene objects, and the information popover presents additional product details and a purchase action outside the 3D object. Keep critical instructions legible independently of scene perspective while allowing secondary information on request.

## Recovery and lighting

The relocalization example asks people to return to the previous area and leaves Start Over available. It demonstrates both a recovery direction and a way out of an unsuccessful recovery. The paired bright/dim room scenes show why low light can reduce environmental understanding; explain a concrete remedy rather than suggesting that the person merely wait.

## AR identity and spacing

The supplied AR glyph can accompany View in AR on a launch button. Collection badges have glyph-plus-AR and compact glyph-only forms; the expanded form is preferred when it fits. The product-grid example places badges consistently at the same corner without hiding the object.

The clear-space diagrams mark a protected perimeter around the glyph and both badge forms. The source rule is at least one tenth of the asset's height, retained in the main guide. Pink framing and checkerboard transparency in these diagrams are explanatory aids, not artwork to reproduce. Light/dark examples preserve recognizable silhouette, placement, and spacing while changing surrounding contrast.
