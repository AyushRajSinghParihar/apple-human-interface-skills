# iPhone Duo visual examples

Source: [Apple HIG designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo).

## Preserve the task while expanding

The Home Screen and Mail comparisons show a narrow outer display and a wider
inner display serving the same device experience. Mail retains the selected
message while the larger space reveals its message list alongside it. Resizing
can expose another level of the existing hierarchy without changing the task or
discarding its state.

The anatomy diagrams distinguish the visible outer camera near an edge from the
inner camera and central fold. The pose sketches include flat, partially folded,
and supported arrangements. Treat these as changing available regions for the
same interface, rather than six separately designed applications.

## Keep the fold clear

Camera and folding-region diagrams identify space that controls and content must
accommodate. In the Notes comparison, the fully open display gives the note more
width than its list; when partially folded, the two panes become equal in width
on either side of the fold. Preserve readable content and useful controls through
these modest width changes instead of letting the hinge cut through them.

The arrangement diagrams contrast two peer regions beside one another with a
primary region layered over a full-sized secondary region. Choose the container
whose relationship already matches the content; navigation remains outside it.

## Preserve control relationships

The vertical-control diagram orders the Dynamic Island and status information
above the navigation/action toolbar and tab destinations. The multitasking
diagram places each app's controls at its own outside edge. In Mail, list-specific
controls stay over the list while message-specific controls sit beside the
message: proximity still explains scope.

The Calculator comparison keeps the same keypad's actions while reflowing them
into fewer, wider rows on the short display. A full-width, non-scrolling interface
can use its available shape without preserving a portrait grid mechanically.

The compression diagrams show two different priorities. A navigation-led view
retains its destinations and moves toolbar actions into overflow; a task-led view
retains its tools and collapses the tab bar. Preserve the controls central to the
current activity, while keeping the remaining actions reachable.
