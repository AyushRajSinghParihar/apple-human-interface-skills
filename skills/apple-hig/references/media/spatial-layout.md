# Spatial layout visual examples

Source: [Apple HIG spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout).

## Dynamic and fixed scale

Both diagrams move a window away from its original outlined position. With dynamic
scale, its physical size grows so the frontal comparison retains a similar
apparent size. With fixed scale, the distant window appears smaller inside that
original outline. Choose the appropriate scale behavior for readable interface
content versus content whose physical dimensions should stay meaningful.

The satellite example also changes with distance and viewpoint: its surfaces and
reflections reveal a genuinely three-dimensional object instead of a flat image
that always faces the person. Preserve the spatial relationships and lighting
that help people inspect a volume.

## Placement follows posture

The upright seated person faces a window in their natural viewing direction. For
the reclining person, the window is raised and tilted to face them. Both examples
connect the person's eyes to the center of the content. Position useful content
relative to the person's head and posture, rather than requiring them to sit
upright or look toward a fixed room-height plane.
