# Game controls: lessons from the inspected images

These are original observations of the examples in Apple's [Game controls HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls), inspected on 2026-10-25. Read the [article guide](../../subskills/apple-hig-inputs/references/game-controls.md) for platform qualifications and controller mappings.

The landscape-phone placement diagram makes the lower left and right thumb regions easiest to reach, with the upper middle less convenient. Keep frequent controls within comfortable thumb reach without covering the scene's central action. The movement/camera diagram divides broad left and right input regions; it does not prescribe a narrow visible-button-sized touch area.

The pressed-button example adds a bright halo and stronger fill around the thumb contact. Feedback must remain visible beyond the finger. The moving/resting thumbstick pair changes prominence: a stronger outlined control during movement becomes a faint resting mark. Reduce idle obstruction while preserving discoverability and feedback when interaction begins.

The physical-square-button to virtual-hand-action diagram distinguishes the controller's glyph from the action's meaning. In touch UI, depict the in-game action. The power-up pair uses one flame control for a tap and a sustained hold, with the latter showing surrounding progress; related actions need not become additional tiny buttons. These are examples, not mandatory game artwork.

The controller anatomy locates triggers and shoulders along the top edge, sticks on the face, and a directional pad below the left stick. Use the active hardware's labels and glyphs; the SF Symbols Gaming category illustrates an available symbol vocabulary rather than one universal controller layout.
