# Wallet visual examples

Source: [Apple HIG Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet).
These original notes describe inspected still-image examples and their relevant
lessons. The diagrams are schematic; they do not establish additional coordinates.

## Pass hierarchy and artwork

The examples distinguish a prominent journey, offer, event, loyalty balance, or
membership identity. Poster passes can give artwork most of the front, while
ordinary passes use compact fields. Both treatments keep machine-readable codes
and essential readable information available. The good/bad pairs show that a
quiet, darker background can preserve text clarity where equally colorful
high-frequency artwork behind the same labels makes them harder to read.

The logo comparison contrasts a clean flat silhouette with an inset shadow that
obscures its edge. The annotated examples separate ordinary top-leading logos,
primary airline logos, and small secondary event branding near the bottom. A
notification icon and its Wallet representation carry the same recognizable
identity into different system contexts.

Background templates reserve top regions for identity and header information,
and lower regions for the material-backed details and barcode. A QR code uses a
taller central square; a linear barcode needs a broad horizontal area. Place the
art's important subject where these overlays will not hide it. The museum and
waterfall examples demonstrate different artwork with the same need to account
for the actual code shape and template.

## Watch pass layouts

The general diagram has a logo and essential field on its first row, primary
information in its middle, and secondary/auxiliary information below. The
style-specific examples use that hierarchy differently:

| Style | Top beside the logo | Middle | Lower information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boarding | Departure or boarding time | Journey origin and destination | Passenger and seat |
| Coupon | Expiration | Strip artwork | Unused |
| Store card | Unused | Strip artwork | Member name and number |
| Event ticket | Event date | Event, teams, or venue | Guest and seating |
| Generic | Expiration | Strip artwork | Name and number |

The selected-card screenshot and opened-details screenshot show how the compact
journey summary leads to additional information and a larger code. Preserve
essential information through both presentations rather than relying on a
phone-only field or artwork region.

## Orders and verification

Order examples distinguish placed, arriving, pickup, and delivered states. Their
annotated layout identifies merchant identity, status or delivery date, a short
status explanation, tracking link, and item name/image/quantity/price. The pickup
example separates a barcode and collection instructions from shipping content.
The contacts sheet presents distinct communication methods rather than burying
support in an unrelated screen. The product-image example confirms a square
300-pixel asset with a simple background.

Verification buttons use different action language for age, identity, continuing,
and general verification. Their light/dark examples preserve the Wallet identity
and action meaning while adapting the border/background treatment. Pass and order
appearance pairs likewise retain hierarchy and data while changing system colors;
sample names, prices, dates, and branding are examples, not required content.
