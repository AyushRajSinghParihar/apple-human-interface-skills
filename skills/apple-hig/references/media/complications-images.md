# Complication visual examples

Source: [Apple HIG Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications). Snapshot: 2026-10-25.

These original notes describe the inspected template examples. The [main guide](../../subskills/apple-hig-system-experiences/references/complications.md) retains device-specific dimensions and placeholder requirements. The displayed readings, times, cities, and colors illustrate possible data; they are not required values.

## Circular and extra-large graphic forms

A closed gauge surrounds a centered symbol or number with a complete ring track. The colored portion expresses progress against that track. An open gauge leaves a gap below the reading: that gap can contain a small symbol, a short descriptor, or two endpoint values. The illustrated open ranges also use a marker to locate the current value along the arc. Keep the central value distinct from its endpoint values.

An image-only circular form gives the entire disk to recognizable artwork. The stack alternatives put a symbol above a value, or a short text identifier above another value. The bezel example adds text around the upper outside of a date disk; it does not put a long paragraph inside the circle. The extra-large graphic examples preserve these relationships at larger sizes rather than adding a different information hierarchy.

## Corner and inline forms

Corner examples follow the watch-face edge. They include a circular image, an edge-following gauge with a small symbol below, a gauge with a current reading and endpoint labels, two curved text lines, and a curved value with an associated symbol. Their curved presentation needs a template designed for the slot; a flat rectangular label is a different form.

Small utilitarian examples offer flat short text, a small standalone image, or a ring around an image or number. Ring examples show both an open and a closed track. The large utilitarian example is a single broad line combining a time and event description. Keep this distinction between a compact corner item and a longer inline strip.

## Rectangular forms

The standard-body example places a colored title above two supporting lines, with decreasing emphasis. The text-gauge example combines a title/symbol, a readable amount, and a horizontal progress track. The image example uses a prominent recent measurement above a time-series chart whose axes are quieter. Use the rectangular area to explain a changing condition rather than merely enlarging a logo.

## Legacy circular small

The six examples distinguish a ring around an image, a ring around a number, a standalone image, a large short value, an image above a value, and two stacked text lines. Their centered compact silhouettes suit the circular slot; the same design must still meet that legacy template's smaller dimensions.

## Legacy modular small

A two-column example aligns short identifiers with their values on two rows. Other alternatives use an image or number inside a ring, a standalone image or value, or stacked image/text and text/text. Use aligned columns when the relationship between pairs matters; use a stack when a symbol or short heading identifies one main reading.

## Legacy modular large

The column example aligns three metric labels against current/goal values. The standard-body example gives a location title, current conditions, and supporting high/low values. A table example uses a heading above two score/name rows. The tall-body example makes a date much larger than its weekday label. These show different hierarchies within the same broad slot; choose the one that makes the important information immediately readable.

## Legacy extra large

The examples repeat ring-image, ring-number, image-only, short-value-only, image-over-value, and text-over-value arrangements at much larger size. Preserve their simple focus rather than filling the larger area with unrelated detail. Their image and stack dimensions remain specific to the extra-large template.
